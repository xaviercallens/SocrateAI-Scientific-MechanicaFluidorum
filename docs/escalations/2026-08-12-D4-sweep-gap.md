## What happened

The prescribed timestep rule `dt = 0.1 / (ν·k_N² + k_N)` with `k_N = 2^N` collapses as `N`
grows: at `N = 12, ν = 0.1` it yields `dt ≈ 5.9e-8`, requiring ~1.7×10⁸ steps for `T = 10`.
The agent correctly refused to run these, marking them `INFEASIBLE` rather than silently
truncating or fabricating — **the right behavior**, and the reason this is an anomaly report
rather than a misconduct report.

**Coverage: 15 of 36 configurations completed (21 INFEASIBLE).**

| N | ν=0.1 | ν=0.01 | ν=0.001 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | OK | OK | OK |
| 12 | INFEASIBLE | OK | OK |
| 16 | INFEASIBLE | INFEASIBLE | INFEASIBLE |
| 20 | INFEASIBLE | INFEASIBLE | INFEASIBLE |

So the sweep varies the cutoff over **only `N ∈ {8, 12}`, and only at two viscosities**.

## Why this matters scientifically

The entire purpose of D4 was to steer D5 — the cutoff-uniformity question, which is the
dyadic analogue of Hypothesis U. A two-point comparison in `N`, at the low end of the range,
**cannot distinguish saturation from slow growth**, which is precisely the distinction the
program needs. The recorded numbers must not be read as evidence either way.

**No verdict is issued here** (PLAN.md §8). Two independent reasons the apparent agreement
must not be called uniformity: (i) `N ∈ {8,12}` is two points, and the dyadic modes above the
initially excited ones are barely populated at these viscosities, so higher shells may simply
be inactive rather than bounded; (ii) these are float64 results — Tier C — and the harness has
no error control, so agreement at the 8th digit may be numerical rather than physical.

## Smallest question that unblocks D5

Which of these is the intended design?

1. **Fixed-cutoff-independent stepping** — choose `dt` from the *active* dynamics (e.g. from
   ν and the initially excited shells) rather than from `k_N`, so that increasing `N` costs
   only more arithmetic per step, not more steps. This makes `N ∈ {8,…,24}` feasible and is
   the standard approach for stiff shell models.
2. **Implicit/exponential integrator** for the linear viscous term (which is what forces the
   tiny explicit `dt`), with explicit treatment of the nonlinearity only.
3. **Accept the narrow range** and design D5's certified scheme to work at small `N` only,
   acknowledging that the uniformity question then goes unanswered by this route.

Option 1 or 2 is required before D5's certified-integration design memo
(`docs/designs/D5-certified-integration.md`) can be written. This is a design decision, not a
mechanical fix, so it is escalated rather than patched.
